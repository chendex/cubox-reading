The Quiet Cost of Constant Interruption

持续被打断的隐性代价

We rarely notice the cost of an interruption because it is paid in a currency we do not track. A notification arrives, we glance at it, and then we return to what we were doing. On paper the exchange takes eleven seconds. In practice it takes much longer, because the mind does not resume from the exact place it stopped. It resumes from wherever it drifted to, and the drift is invisible to the person doing the drifting.

我们很少注意到打断带来的代价，因为它是以一种我们不追踪的货币支付的。一条通知来了，我们瞥一眼，然后回到刚才在做的事。纸面上这次交换只花十一秒。实际上要长得多，因为大脑不会从它停下的确切位置恢复。它从漂移到的任何地方恢复，而这种漂移对正在漂移的人来说是不可见的。

Psychologists describe this as attention residue: a portion of your working memory keeps processing the previous task even after you have switched. The residue decays, but it competes with the new task for the same limited resources. When you interrupt a piece of deep work six times in an hour, you are not doing one hour of work with six brief pauses. You are doing six shallow fragments, each one starting from a colder position than the last.

心理学家将此描述为注意力残留：即使你已经切换了任务，你工作记忆的一部分仍在处理之前的任务。残留会衰减，但它会与新任务争夺同样的有限资源。如果你在一小时内打断一项深度工作六次，你并不是在做一小时的工作、中间有六次短暂停顿。你是在做六个浅层碎片，每一个都从比上一个更冷的位置开始。

This is why the arithmetic of distraction feels wrong when you try to account for it. People who work in an open office report more interruptions and also report feeling busier, not less productive. The two facts are not contradictory. Interruption generates activity without generating progress, and activity is what our sense of effort is calibrated against.

这就是为什么当你试图核算分心的算术时会觉得不对劲。在开放式办公室工作的人报告更多的打断，同时也报告感觉更忙，而不是效率更低。这两个事实并不矛盾。打断产生了活动，却没有产生进展，而我们的努力感正是以活动为校准基准的。

Reading is where this becomes easiest to see. A difficult paragraph requires you to hold several claims in mind at once so that you can compare them. If you are pulled away in the middle, the claims dissolve, and you have to rebuild the structure from the beginning. Do that often enough and you stop attempting difficult paragraphs altogether, because the expected reward no longer justifies the expected cost of restarting.

阅读是最容易看到这一点的场景。一个困难的段落要求你同时把几个论点记在脑中，以便进行比较。如果你在中途被拉走，这些论点就消散了，你不得不从头重建结构。这样做得足够频繁，你就会完全不再尝试困难的段落，因为预期的回报不再能证明重新开始的预期成本是合理的。

What follows is not a dramatic collapse but a quiet narrowing. You begin to prefer writing that is already structured for you: listicles, summaries, threads. These are not worse in themselves. They are simply optimized for a different mode of attention, one that can survive interruption without losing anything, because there was nothing fragile to lose.

随之而来的不是戏剧性的崩溃，而是安静的收窄。你开始偏好已经为你组织好结构的写作：清单体、摘要、推文串。这些本身并不更差。它们只是针对另一种注意力模式做了优化，那种模式能在被打断后存活而不损失任何东西，因为本来就没有脆弱的东西可失去。

The counterargument is that this adaptation is efficient. Why fight for depth if skimming delivers most of the value at a fraction of the cost? The answer depends on what you are trying to do with what you read. If you need the shape of an argument, skimming is genuinely better. If you need to disagree with it, you need the argument itself, including the parts the summary dropped because they were inconvenient.

反方观点是这种适应是高效的。如果略读能以一小部分成本带来大部分价值，为什么要为深度而战？答案取决于你想用你读到的东西做什么。如果你需要的是一个论证的轮廓，略读确实更好。如果你需要不同意它，你需要论证本身，包括摘要因为不方便而丢掉的部分。

Annotation is the cheapest tool we have for this. Writing a margin note forces you to state, in your own words, what the author claimed. That restatement is a test: if you cannot produce it, you did not understand the sentence, you merely recognized the words. Recognition feels like comprehension and is not.

批注是我们为此拥有的最便宜的工具。写一条页边注迫使你用自己的话陈述作者的主张。那个复述是一个测试：如果你无法产出它，你就没有理解那句话，你只是认出了那些词。识别感觉像理解，但并不是。

There is a second benefit that is easy to miss. Notes are portable across time in a way that reading is not. Six months later you will not remember the article, but you will remember why you disagreed with it, because the disagreement was yours and the article was theirs. The note is the part that survives.

还有第二个好处容易被忽略。笔记可以跨时间携带，而阅读不能。六个月后你不会记得那篇文章，但你会记得你为什么不同意它，因为不同意是你的，文章是他们的。笔记是幸存下来的那部分。

Practical advice tends to collapse into abstinence: turn off notifications, close the tabs, use a different browser profile. These work, but they treat the symptom as a moral failing. The deeper issue is that most information systems are designed to be interrupted, because interruption is how they measure engagement. You are not weak for responding to a machine built to elicit the response.

实用建议往往坍缩为戒断：关掉通知，关闭标签页，使用不同的浏览器配置文件。这些有用，但它们把症状当作道德缺陷来对待。更深层的问题是，大多数信息系统被设计成可被打断的，因为打断是它们衡量参与度的方式。你对一台被造来引发反应的机器做出反应，并不是你软弱。

A more durable strategy is to make the cost visible. Every time you leave a difficult paragraph, note it. After a week you will have a list of the specific things that pulled you away, and the list will be shorter than you expect. Most interruptions come from three or four sources, and those sources are easier to change than your character.

更持久的策略是让成本变得可见。每次你离开一个难处理的段落时，把它记下来。一周之后，你就会得到一份把你拉走的具体事项清单，而这份清单会比你预想的更短。大多数干扰来自三四个来源，而这些来源比你的性格更容易改变。

The final point is about patience, which is the resource all of this consumes. Patience is not a personality trait that some people have and others lack. It is what remains when the expected payoff of staying is higher than the expected payoff of leaving. Design the payoff, and the patience follows.

最后一点关乎耐心，而耐心是这一切所消耗的资源。耐心不是某些人与生俱来、另一些人天生缺乏的人格特质。当留下来的预期回报高于离开的预期回报时，剩下的就是耐心。设计好回报，耐心便会随之而来。
